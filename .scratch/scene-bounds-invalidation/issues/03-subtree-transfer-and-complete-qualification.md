# 03: Safe subtree transfer and complete qualification

**What to build:** Public subtree copy and effective reparent invalidate observations with correct destination ownership, and the complete payload/membership/transfer capability is qualified together before downstream bounds work can rely on it.

**Blocked by:** 02, 15
**Status:** ready-for-human

**Specification:** [Automatic Scene-Bounds Invalidation and Lifetime-Safe Ownership](../spec.md), full Implementation and Testing Decisions.

## Current behavior

Public subtree reparent updates precise topology history but does not necessarily invalidate whole-Scene observations without a signal. Subtree copying constructs payloads from the source; blindly retaining the source Layer association is not qualified destination ownership. Supported same-Scene cross-Layer copies, subsequent source destruction and edits to the copy need explicit behavioral proof.

Read completed01 and02 before starting.02 transitively includes01's lifetime/attachment foundation and payload behavior. Copies use that insertion/membership machinery and reparents change its membership relationships. This is a new public transfer capability with a cumulative gate, not a tests-only final ticket.

## Desired behavior and acceptance criteria

- [x] Effective public subtree reparent automatically invalidates both SceneRevision and InspectionRevision. Reparent away/back without an intermediate read cannot revive old validity. Preserve existing root, cycle, invalid-operation and no-op restrictions; no-op behavior remains unchanged. Do not gain coarse render/UI callbacks or require manual signals.
- [x] Public subtree copying automatically invalidates destination dependencies. Cover leaf/nonleaf copies within a Layer and actually supported cross-Layer copies within one Scene. Copied nodes bind to their receiving Layer, have distinct node lifetimes and preserve appropriate payload/default/SRT/instance values. Subsequent source mutation/removal/destruction cannot corrupt the copy, its resolution or its destination attachment.
- [x] Subsequent edits to copied destination nodes invalidate the correct Scene through01; removal/reuse of copies invalidates through02. Preserve precise payload histories, ordered ancestor/topology change-back semantics, surviving lifetimes through reparent/reserve relocation and exact object-use counts. Do not confuse cloning, attached assignment, genuine move-out and storage relocation. No cross-Scene object-index reinterpretation or invented unsupported transfer API.
- [x] Reuse the single lifetime-qualified validity mechanism: fixed scene-local bookkeeping, O(1) coherent snapshots/comparisons, both getter orders, stable polling, independent Scenes, no fingerprints/extra Scene walks/growing per-query history. Preserve01/02 owner expiry and callback/batching guarantees across transfers, including copies followed by source and destination owner destruction and retained Layers/payloads.
- [x] TDD real public copy/reparent operations with independently expected hierarchy, values, object counts and node identities. Include nonleaf cloning, cross-Layer copies followed by source destruction, edits to the destination, reparent change-away/back, rejected/no-op/cycle/root controls, copy/removal/reuse, reserve relocation, getter order and unchanged callbacks/batching/precise histories. Generic Forest/Data Tree behavior remains unchanged in its default configuration.

No ownership, failure, gap, root, assignment/move, helper/direct or destructive operation criterion remains silently deferred to downstream bounds implementation.

After a previously successful synchronization, edits and subsequent failed synchronization do not revive old revisions. CPU-side dependency validity does not require a ready RenderIndex. Existing admitted operation authority/payload suppression and retained historical replay remain unchanged; historical results keep their original revisions rather than being relabeled current.
